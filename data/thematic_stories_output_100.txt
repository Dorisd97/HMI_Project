 Title: The Dark Dealings Within Enron's Energy Empire: A Tale of Deception and Collusion

   Actors:

   - California Independent System Operator (California ISO)
   - Duke Energy North America
   - Bill Lockyer (California Attorney General)
   - Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC)

   Story:
   In the heart of December 1999, amidst the chilly winds of corporate America, key figures within Enron Corporation were engaging in a complex dance of energy transactions and negotiations.

   Martin Rosell initiated a system shutdown for necessary maintenance on December 7, signaling the intricate web of operations underway. Meanwhile, Cathy Bretz was working diligently on updates regarding the Gas OII settlement with PG&E, ensuring that all parties were kept informed and concerns addressed to produce a revised tariff draft by May 25.

   As April 1, 2000 approached, Frank invited Jeff to join Project Boomerang, a clandestine California energy project involving gas transportation, storage, and balancing changes. The project was shrouded in mystery, with Frank conducting due diligence on California QFs purchased by El Paso Merchant Energy from Dynegy and investigating a lawsuit filed under Section 5 of AB1890 for further scrutiny.

   Simultaneously, Enron executives were grappling with the intricacies of power plant interconnections and transmission obligations. The need for careful review of VEPCO's interconnection agreement was emphasized due to potential emergency power requirements and associated costs. Enron's inability to use VEPCO's network transmission to deliver to third parties added another layer of complexity, necessitating a request for transmission on OASIS to guarantee deliverability.

   Amidst this labyrinthine web of energy transactions, California Attorney General Bill Lockyer launched an investigation into alleged collusion in the wholesale electricity market. Energy giants like Duke Energy North America were being scrutinized, with denials of wrongdoing and welcoming of the probe. The findings from this investigation, to be submitted by the end of the year, would prove to be a turning point in the saga of Enron's dark dealings.

   Conclusion:
   As the curtain fell on 2000, the true extent of Enron's machinations remained shrouded in mystery. However, the investigation by California Attorney General Bill Lockyer would ultimately expose a web of deceit and collusion, leading to the corporation's downfall. The lasting impact of this scandal served as a stark reminder of the importance of transparency and accountability in corporate America.
